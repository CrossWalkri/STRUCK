# STRUCK-to-CRAFT Condition Map

CRAFT depends on ORE and STRUCK; neither depends on CRAFT. CRAFT's six conditions discipline the evaluation chain an operator builds, and a complete CRAFT certification is not whole without an account of what enters the chain at ingestion and what it hands over at exit. This document is the worked account of what CRAFT gets from STRUCK at each of the six conditions, at the exit boundary, onto the face of the output the chain hands to whoever acts on it. It is the mirror, at the other end, of the ORE-to-CRAFT condition map. As of CRAFT v0.4.5, a chain whose output is consumed outside it carries an exit boundary declaration (CRAFT Section 6.1), satisfied by adopting STRUCK or by declaring equivalent obligations; this map shows, condition by condition, what that adoption gives CRAFT.

The map is descriptive. Nothing here adds obligations beyond what the two specifications state; where a mapping suggests a future obligation, it is marked as a direction, not a requirement.

## Condition 1: Decision Context Specification

CRAFT's frame element names the decision, the parties, the population, and what is being estimated, before any data is collected. STRUCK is where that frame leaves the chain intact: an output carries what it was built and tested for, so a consumer can judge whether their use matches the use it was made for. STRUCK certifies that an output met its prestated intended use; it is the exit-side reading of Condition 1's decision context, not a re-judgment of it.

CRAFT's risk reduction statement identifies who bears the harm if the chain is wrong. STRUCK's obligations exist for that risk-bearer at the far end: the graded profile, the refutation conditions, the kept contest, and the walked derivation are all carried on the face of the output so the party who acts can see what they are standing on. STRUCK's worth obligation leaves the judgment of sufficiency to that party rather than asserting it for them; the exposure exists for the consumer of the output, never for the producer.

CRAFT's adversarial threat model names actors who satisfy the conditions while defeating the purpose. The exit-shaped member of that class is the output that is accurate and structurally unusable: true on its face, but carrying no way to be checked, so it travels faster than its own verification. A threat model that omits its exit boundary has omitted the route by which a sound chain still ships an uncheckable claim.

Role in one line: STRUCK carries Condition 1's declarations out the back door, the output states what it was for and leaves the worth-call to the named consumer.

## Condition 2: Technical Ontology Adequate to the Decision Context

CRAFT requires known gaps in the ontology to be stated, because unstated gaps become invisible failure modes indistinguishable from valid output. STRUCK's conformance discipline is the same rule at the exit: an output carries all five obligations or explicitly declares and justifies the absence of any that does not apply, and silence about an obligation is not conformance. No silent gaps in what the output exposes about what it rests on.

STRUCK's graded-evidence obligation carries the stated-gaps discipline onto the output's face: the profile reports the weakest assessment present anywhere in the support together with every flag any source carries, so a dimension that could not be assessed leaves the chain as a named limitation rather than a silent one. A direction the two specifications share, not yet an obligation in either: an output declaring the temporal scope of its constructs, present-state, predictive, or historical, so a present-state reading cannot be mistaken for a predictive one on the far side of the boundary.

Role in one line: STRUCK is the stated-gaps discipline for what the output carries, the weakest dimension and every flag exposed, absent obligations declared rather than omitted.

## Condition 3: Valid Measurement Instruments within the Ontology

CRAFT makes uncertainty quantification a carried requirement on every instrument and requires systematic error sources to be named. Inside the chain, that uncertainty lives in the evaluation record; STRUCK is what puts it on the face of the output when the output leaves. The graded-evidence obligation is the instrument's uncertainty budget travelling with the claim: the reading's weakest supporting dimension, its flags, and its unassessable regions arrive with the output rather than staying behind in the chain that produced it.

STRUCK's refusal to combine the profile into a single confidence figure is the exit form of CRAFT's requirement that systematic and random error be kept distinct: a single number collapses the distinction the instrument was required to preserve, and it is easy to tune and hard to audit at exactly the moment the output is being trusted.

Role in one line: STRUCK carries every instrument's uncertainty budget onto the face of the output, so the reading's uncertainty travels with the claim.

## Condition 4: Pre-Specified Evaluation Criteria

CRAFT requires criteria specified before data is observed, because criteria adjusted to the data optimize for coherent appearance. STRUCK's refutation obligation is the exit expression of that discipline: the output states, in advance and in the producer's own words, what would overturn it, in terms of what could be found in the world rather than in terms of internal process. Pre-specified criteria say what would count as passing; refutation conditions say what would count as being wrong, and both must be fixed before the answer is in hand rather than fitted to it after.

CRAFT's gaming clause observes that stated criteria are optimized against under incentive. STRUCK's bar against refutation conditions set at a threshold the producer knows to be unreachable is the same insight at the boundary: a condition written so no available instrument could ever satisfy it is a refusal to be checked, dressed as an invitation.

Role in one line: STRUCK carries the criteria out as refutation conditions, the output states in advance and in world terms what would count as its being wrong.

## Condition 5: Explicit Decision Logic

CRAFT requires the logic by which outputs produce decisions to exist as a written specification, independently of the code that implements it. STRUCK is where the boundary keeps that logic honest: the worth obligation leaves the judgment of sufficiency to the party consuming the output, and requires the output to carry what that party needs to make it. The combining of the output into an action is Condition 5's business, and STRUCK refuses to pre-empt it, an output MUST NOT assert that its support is adequate to any particular decision, because whether it suffices depends on what is being decided and what being wrong would cost, neither of which the producer generally knows.

The rule is symmetric for human and automated consumers. Where the consumer is automated, STRUCK requires the decision logic that composes outputs into an action to be itself readable and challengeable, so the judgment is made from the readings rather than in silence. This is the exit mirror of ORE's Condition 5 attachment: ORE delivers the decision logic legible ingredients at ingestion, STRUCK keeps them legible on the way out, and Condition 5 is where the combining in between becomes accountable.

Role in one line: STRUCK delivers the decision's legible ingredients at the output boundary and leaves the worth-judgment, Condition 5's combining, to the consumer, made from the readings rather than in silence.

## Condition 6: Feedback Mechanism with Propagation

CRAFT requires detected errors to propagate to every prior stage. Propagation can only reach a stage that has an address, and STRUCK's derivation obligation is what gives the output one: every rung of support is walked to ultimate origin and labeled for what it is, so an error found in the output can be traced to the rung it entered at rather than dead-ending in an unattributed claim. Where ORE is Condition 6's propagation target inside the chain, STRUCK is what makes an already-released output still traceable once it has left.

STRUCK's kept-contest obligation is the feedback discipline applied to a resolution: a losing position retained in the record is a resolution that can be revisited when new evidence arrives, where a losing position silently dropped cannot be. A direction the specifications share, not yet an obligation in either: a released output that is later overturned propagating that reversal to the parties who received it, the exit analogue of re-grading a source.

Role in one line: STRUCK's labeled derivation is the forwarding address that lets Condition 6's propagation reach a released output, and kept contest is what lets a resolution be revisited.

## What this map is not

It is not an inheritance receipt: STRUCK is a companion specification, not a CRAFT domain application, and carries no receipt. It is not normative: each obligation described here binds through its home specification, and a conflict between this map and either specification is resolved in the specification's favor. And it is not complete for the future: the directions marked above (temporal-scope exposure, reversal propagation to recipients) belong to the specifications' own next versions, where they will be obligations or will not exist.
